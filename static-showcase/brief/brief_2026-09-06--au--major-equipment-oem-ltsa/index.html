Major Equipment OEM & LTSA · Australia (Perth)

Strengthen LTSA and OEM Specs for Networks and Muting Risks

Published Sep 6, 2026, 6:08 AM AWST•APAC•Full category signal

Decision at a glance

Top move

Process Online published a practical piece showing 'muting' can introduce safety vulnerabilities; buyers should treat muting implementations as actionable acceptance items rather than vendor claims

Key takeaways

  • Process Online published a practical piece showing 'muting' can introduce safety vulnerabilities; buyers should treat muting implementations as actionable acceptance items rather than vendor claims.[1]
  • New coverage on industrial networks highlights IEC 62443-3-3 and certified EtherCAT paths — this makes cybersecurity and network-conformance concrete contract levers for OEMs and long-term service agreements (LTSA).[2]
  • A Bonfiglioli case study shows local gearboxes and ancillary equipment upgrades are being specified and supplied for advanced processing sites — treat supplier local capability and scope clarity as negotiation points for LTSA placement.[3]

Why this matters for the category manager

Category impact

Cost / money

  • Including recorded commissioning and muting configuration in acceptance criteria can shift remediation costs from operations budgets into supplier liability under LTSA, tightening buyer cost exposure control.[1]
  • Requiring IEC 62443 conformance and network acceptance testing creates upfront scope and testing cost but reduces downstream reactive cyber‑remediation spend during LTSA periods.[2]

Supplier / commercial

  • Local OEMs or suppliers demonstrating recent project delivery (e.g., gearboxes in Wyong) have leverage for longer-term service placements unless buyers lock scope, mobilization windows and spare-part commitments.[3]
  • Suppliers may narrow quote validity or exclude commissioning if buyers demand recorded FAT/SAT and cyber evidence; plan negotiation positions accordingly.[1]

Safety / operations

  • Muting practices can create latent safety gaps if commissioning artifacts and muting parameters are not recorded and contractually accepted; operations should insist on measurable acceptance artifacts.[1][2]
  • Network-dependent sensors and gateways increase operational dependency on secure remote access and recovery procedures; absence of tested rollback plans raises uptime risk under LTSA.[2]

What to watch

  • Vendor-written how-to and product pieces dominate the source; treat claims about AI, edge compute, or network performance as directional until validated with FAT/SAT artifacts.[1]
  • Standards references (IEC 62443, EtherCAT certification) are actionable but may be claimed at varying assurance levels — verify certificates and test reports rather than accepting labelling.[2]
