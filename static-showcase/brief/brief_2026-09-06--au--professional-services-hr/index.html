Adjust Sourcing to Trust Tax and Advisory Workload Signals

Published Sep 6, 2026, 6:10 AM AWST•APAC•Full category signal

Decision at a glance

Top move

Published minimum-tax and related tax coverage is creating tangible extra advisory work for trust and property matters; buyers should treat this as an operational demand signal for advisory firms

Key takeaways

  • Published minimum-tax and related tax coverage is creating tangible extra advisory work for trust and property matters; buyers should treat this as an operational demand signal for advisory firms.[2]
  • Supplier differentiation is shifting to packaged services, documented standard operating procedures (SOPs) and hiring discipline — firms that productize advisory work are easier to scale and manage.[1]
  • Moving property and valuation work off spreadsheets is now a visible operational theme: that drives short-term integration, onboarding and potential licensing or implementation costs for buyers.[3]

Why this matters for the category manager

Published minimum-tax and related tax coverage is creating tangible extra advisory work for trust and property matters; buyers should treat this as an operational demand signal for advisory firms. Supplier differentiation is shifting to packaged services, documented standard operating procedures (SOPs) and hiring discipline — firms that productize advisory work are easier to scale and manage. Moving property and valuation work off spreadsheets is now a visible operational theme: that drives short-term integration, onboarding and potential licensing or implementation costs for buyers. Expect suppliers to respond commercially (narrower quote windows, mobilisation premiums, or requests to pass through costs); this is already a market conversation rather than pure speculation

What changed

  • Accountants Daily has added explicit coverage naming the minimum tax for discretionary trusts, increasing clarity that trust-related advisory workload will rise (new since prior brief).
  • A profile of a fast-scaling advisory-led firm makes documented SOPs and packaged services a visible commercial differentiator compared with five weeks ago.

Category impact

Cost / money

  • Upward fee pressure is likely where suppliers must do detailed trust deed reviews, valuation modelling or produce evidence trails; these tasks increase hours and documentation effort.[2]
  • Buyers may see a shift from simple hourly engagements toward suppliers pushing fixed-fee or packaged pricing that embeds automation or platform fees.[1]

Supplier / commercial

  • Productised suppliers with clear SOWs and SOPs will win selection and can preserve margins; expect them to resist pure time-and-materials procurement approaches.[1]
  • Smaller or capacity-constrained practices may narrow quote acceptance windows or introduce mobilisation premiums to protect schedules — creating new negotiation levers for buyers.[2]

Safety / operations

  • Reliance on spreadsheets for property and valuation work increases error and rework risk; buyers should treat migration readiness as an operational control.[3]
  • Reports of fraud targeting tax professionals underscore the need to validate supplier cyber and data-handling controls when shifting work or onboarding new advisers.[2][3]

What to watch

  • Watch early pass-through price requests that cite legislative complexity without a time-and-resource breakdown — some suppliers will use complexity as justification rather than evidence.[2]
  • Watch mid-tier firms advertising rapid migration off spreadsheets; if they cannot back it with integration plans, buyers risk onboarding delays and hidden implementation costs.[3]
